# DIGITAL CODE OF THE REPUBLIC OF KAZAKHSTAN

## Chapter 1. General Provisions

### Article 1. Relations Regulated by this Code

1. This Code governs public relations in the digital environment arising from the creation, processing, storage, and transmission of digital data.
2. Commodity-money and other property-related relations based on the equality of participants, as well as associated personal non-property relations, are governed by the civil legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. Public relations arising from the interaction between business entities and the state, including state regulation and support of entrepreneurship, are governed by the Entrepreneurial Code of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Relations in the field of intellectual property are governed by legislation on copyright and related rights.
5. Foreign nationals, stateless persons, and foreign legal entities enjoy rights and freedoms and bear obligations in relations arising in the digital environment on an equal basis with citizens and legal entities of the Republic of Kazakhstan, unless otherwise provided by this Code, the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, or international treaties ratified by the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 2. Legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan Governing Relations in the Digital Environment

1. The legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan governing relations in the digital environment (hereinafter referred to as “digital legislation”) is based on the Constitution of the Republic of Kazakhstan and consists of this Code and other normative legal acts of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. Where an international treaty ratified by the Republic of Kazakhstan establishes rules different from those contained in this Code, the rules of the international treaty shall apply.
3. In the event of a contradiction between this Code and other laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan containing norms governing relations in the digital environment, the provisions of this Code shall prevail.

### Article 3. Objectives and Tasks of the Digital Legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan

1. The objective of the digital legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan is to define the legal foundations, objectives, principles, and mechanisms for implementing a unified state policy in the digital environment.
2. The tasks of the digital legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan are:
   1) implementation of legal regulation in the digital environment;
   2) maintenance of digital resilience;
   3) ensuring the foundations for sustainable digital development of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   4) creating conditions for attracting investment into the development of digital technologies;
   5) ensuring the fulfillment of international treaty and other obligations of the Republic of Kazakhstan and promoting international cooperation in the field of digital technologies;
   6) fostering digital culture in society, promoting knowledge at all levels of education, and advancing education and public awareness to ensure the sustainable development of digital technologies;
   7) strengthening the rule of law and public order in the application of digital technologies;
   8) digital transformation of public administration.

### Article 4. Principles of Regulation of Relations in the Digital Environment

1. Legal regulation of relations in the digital environment is based on the following principles:
   1) priority of human interests;
   2) ensuring the security of individuals, society, and the state in the digital environment;
   3) inviolability of private life and prohibition of the collection, storage, use, and dissemination of information about a person’s private life without their consent;
   4) freedom to search for, create, and transmit digital records whose access is not restricted under the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   5) freedom to create and use any digital technologies not prohibited by this Code and the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   6) freedom of access to state digital resources and systems whose access is not restricted under the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   7) extraterritoriality of relations in the digital environment;
   8) ensuring a balance between private and public interests;
   9) prevention of digital inequality and restriction of constitutional rights of citizens who lack the ability and/or willingness to use digital technologies;
   10) limitation of state involvement in the creation and operation of digital resources and systems not intended for the provision of public services and/or internal use by state bodies and organizations.

## Chapter 2. Digital State

### Article 5. Coordination of Activities of State Bodies in the Digital Environment

1. The Commission under the President of the Republic of Kazakhstan on Digitalization Issues in the Republic of Kazakhstan (hereinafter referred to as “the Commission”) is an advisory body under the President of the Republic of Kazakhstan responsible for formulating a unified state policy in the digital environment.
2. The Regulations of the Commission are approved by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan. The composition of the Commission is approved by the President of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. The operating body of the Commission is the authorized body for digitalization and cybersecurity (hereinafter referred to as “the authorized body”).
4. The Expert Council on Digitalization is an advisory body under the authorized body that carries out cross-sectoral and inter-agency coordination of state bodies’ activities in the digital environment, excluding special state bodies and the Ministry of Defense of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. The Regulations of the Expert Council on Digitalization and its composition are approved by the authorized body.
6. The opinion of the Expert Council on Digitalization shall be mandatorily taken into account when planning budget expenditures for digitalization.
7. The Special Expert Council is an advisory body established by the National Security Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan, the State Security Service, and the Ministry of Defense of the Republic of Kazakhstan. The operating body of the Council is the National Security Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
8. The Regulations of the Special Expert Council and its composition are approved by a joint order of the heads of the special state bodies of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
9. The conclusions of the Special Expert Council serve as the basis for planning budget expenditures for the creation and development of state digital systems of special state bodies.

### Article 6. Digital Transformation of Public Administration

1. Digital transformation of public administration is a process of systemic changes in the activities of state bodies, based on the introduction of digital technologies, architectural and platform-based solutions, data analytics, and artificial intelligence, aimed at improving the efficiency, transparency, proactiveness of governance, and delivery of public services.
2. The objectives of the digital transformation of public administration are:
   1) building a digital state through the modernization of digital infrastructure and transition to platform-based architectural solutions;
   2) improving channels of interaction among parties in the digital environment by reducing the number of procedures and eliminating redundant administrative actions;
   3) introducing algorithmic and automated mechanisms for regulation and execution of public administration functions;
   4) ensuring data-driven and predictive analytics-based decision-making;
   5) transitioning public services to a digital and proactive format;
   6) full digitization of data with the phased elimination of paper-based documentation;
   7) eliminating repeated requests for information from individuals and legal entities when such information is already contained in digital objects of the digital government;
   8) ensuring accessibility, fault tolerance, and inclusiveness of digital services while complying with digital rights and personal data protection standards.
3. All state bodies of the Republic of Kazakhstan are required to develop and implement digital transformation programs for their activities, including the modernization of internal processes, information systems, service delivery approaches, and data management.
4. The level of digital transformation of a state body is determined by the digital maturity indicator.
5. Cross-sectoral coordination of the digital transformation of public administration is carried out by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.

### Article 7. Digital Maturity

1. Digital maturity is an indicator reflecting the level of digital development and readiness of an organization, sector, or territory to use digital technologies.
2. Digital maturity demonstrates the degree of institutional, technological, procedural, and personnel transformation that ensures effective implementation and application of digital solutions.
3. The digital maturity of a state body reflects:
   1) the existence and implementation of architectural and organizational solutions provided for in the digital transformation program;
   2) the level of process automation and algorithmization;
   3) the degree of integration of digital services, including inter-agency interaction;
   4) the quality of data management;
   5) the development of staff’s digital competencies;
   6) compliance with digital rights;
   7) conformity with unified platform and technical standards.
4. Digital maturity shall be regularly assessed in the manner determined by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.
5. The results of the digital maturity assessment are used for planning and adjusting digital development strategies, allocating budget funds for digital transformation, evaluating the effectiveness of state body leadership, monitoring the implementation of state programs, and deploying artificial intelligence systems.

### Article 8. Digitalization Development Institute

1. The Digitalization Development Institute is a legal entity designated by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan to provide scientific, analytical, methodological, and project support for digital transformation in the country.
2. The Digitalization Development Institute performs the following functions:
   1) development and updating of digital transformation programs at the national and sectoral levels;
   2) formulation of proposals on the architecture of the digital government and platform-based solutions;
   3) preparation of methodologies, standards, indicators, and models for assessing digital maturity;
   4) organizational and methodological support for the digital transformation of state and quasi-state sector bodies;
   5) conducting scientific and applied research in the field of digital development, including data, artificial intelligence, trusted infrastructure, and digital rights;
   6) monitoring and analytics of digital transformation progress, preparation of annual reports and recommendations for authorized bodies.
3. The activities of the Digitalization Development Institute are carried out in accordance with its charter, coordinated with the authorized body, and funded in the manner established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 9. Digital Government

1. Digital government is a public administration system based on the comprehensive use of digital technologies, digital government architecture, and platform-based solutions to effectively, transparently, and proactively perform state functions, deliver public services, and facilitate interaction among legal subjects in the digital environment.
2. The State Platform of the Digital Government is a centralized digital infrastructure that enables the development, deployment, and operation of state digital objects, as well as access to them by state bodies, individuals, and legal entities for the purpose of performing state functions and delivering public services in digital format.
3. The operation of the digital government is aimed at:
   1) ensuring access by individuals and legal entities to publicly available information on the activities of state bodies;
   2) providing automated access by state bodies to information contained in the digital systems of other state bodies;
   3) automating the processes of performing state functions and delivering services;
   4) using digital document management in the performance of state functions and provision of public services;
   5) eliminating duplication in the collection, accumulation, and storage of state digital resources;
   6) ensuring information security, protection of digital objects, and operational resilience of the digital government.
4. The digital government is formed and developed in accordance with the architecture of the digital government, which includes:
   1) description of digital government components, including digital objects;
   2) models of digital execution of functions and service delivery;
   3) allocation of powers and responsibilities among participants in digital interaction;
   4) cross-cutting requirements for data management and cybersecurity.
5. The architecture of the digital government is approved by the authorized body and is subject to regular review in light of technological developments, legislative changes, and administrative reforms.
6. The development of the digital government is carried out in accordance with unified requirements in the fields of digitalization and cybersecurity.
7. The use of the State Platform of the Digital Government is permitted exclusively for the purposes of performing state functions, delivering public services, and digital inter-agency interaction.
8. State bodies and other entities authorized to provide public services are not entitled to request from individuals or legal entities information already contained in the digital government, except in cases provided for by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
9. The procedure for the formation, operation, development, and modernization of the digital government, including architectural, technical, and organizational requirements, is established by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 10. National Registers

1. Authoritative data sources by sector (area) within the framework of the digital government architecture are recognized as national registers.
2. The list of national registers is determined by the authorized body in coordination with the Bureau of National Statistics.
3. Where data are contained in a national register, state digital systems using such data shall, as a priority, obtain them from the relevant register.
4. State bodies shall ensure the appropriate quality of data contained in national registers in accordance with national data quality standards approved by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan and the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. The development of national data quality standards and monitoring of their implementation are carried out by the Bureau of National Statistics of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

## Chapter 3. Objects of Legal Relations in the Digital Environment

### Article 11. Types of Objects of Legal Relations in the Digital Environment

1. Objects of legal relations in the digital environment (digital objects) include:
   1) digital records;
   2) digital resources;
   3) digital systems and platforms;
   4) digital infrastructure objects;
   5) other digital objects provided for by this Code.
2. Digital records, resources, systems, and platforms are recognized as property. In matters not regulated by this Code, civil legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan applies to relations concerning the circulation of digital objects.
3. Digital rights may be established on digital objects, as well as on other property, representing a digital format for the fixation of civil rights. Digital rights to digital objects may also include the right of access to the digital object and the right to set conditions for such access. Digital rights are protected by law.
4. Unless otherwise provided by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, digital rights are transferable and may be assigned from one subject of digital relations to another by agreement.
5. Restrictions on digital rights, including the right of free access to objects of digital rights, are established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
6. Compulsory alienation or restriction of digital rights in respect of a specific object is permitted only on the basis of a court decision in cases provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
7. Digital objects owned by the state and not subject to alienation are recognized as national digital objects. The status of national digital objects is established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
8. The creation, development, maintenance, and protection of national digital objects are funded from budgetary resources.
9. Digital objects may be granted the status of trusted digital objects. A trusted digital object is one created and operated within a jurisdiction that ensures an equivalent level of digital regulation and meets the trust criteria established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 12. National Digital Objects

1. National digital objects are digital objects owned by the state, recognized as strategically important for ensuring digital sovereignty, information security, and the resilience of the digital infrastructure of the Republic of Kazakhstan, and not subject to alienation, except in cases expressly provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. National digital objects may include:
   1) state digital registers, databases, and registries;
   2) digital systems and platforms, including key elements of the state digital platform;
   3) digital infrastructure objects ensuring the performance of state functions and the delivery of public services.
3. The list of national digital objects, as well as the procedure for their recognition, registration, creation, operation, modernization, and protection, is determined by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. The creation, development, maintenance, and protection of national digital objects are carried out using state budget funds or other sources not prohibited by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. Transfer of ownership, use, or disposal rights in respect of national digital objects to foreign states, international organizations, or foreign legal entities is prohibited.
6. National digital objects shall not be located, stored, or processed outside the territory of the Republic of Kazakhstan, except in cases provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
7. National digital objects are subject to protection in accordance with the requirements in the fields of digitalization and cybersecurity.

### Article 13. Digital Data

1. Digital data means any information in a formalized form suitable for processing, storage, and transmission, recorded in digital form.
2. Digital data are not objects of civil rights and may be freely created, collected, processed, and transmitted, except in cases established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. Every subject of legal relations in the digital environment has the right to access digital data collected about them, the right to correct errors therein, and the right to have such data deleted if they were collected unlawfully or voluntarily provided by the subject, except in cases established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Digital data relating to individuals that enable their identification are recognized as personal data. The collection, processing, and protection of personal data are governed by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on personal data and their protection.
5. The management of digital data constituting state secrets, official, or confidential information is carried out taking into account the specifics established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
6. State bodies, state legal entities, quasi-state sector entities, and persons authorized by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan to manage certain categories of digital data are required to manage all data they collect and process in accordance with data management requirements approved by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
7. The principles of digital data management in the state and quasi-state sectors are the single-entry principle and the non-duplication principle for digital data.
8. The authorized body for digital data quality is the Bureau of National Statistics of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 14. Digital Records

1. A digital record means a distinct set of digital data that can be identified through a description of its properties and attributes (metadata).
2. Rights to digital records created and obtained by state bodies in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan belong to the state.
3. Digital records used as a virtual commodity, a measure of value, and/or a medium of exchange within specific digital communities, or certifying property rights, are recognized as digital assets and are regulated by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on digital assets.  
Digital assets do not include securities and other digital records certifying property rights as provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Subject to restrictions established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, the owner of digital records holds the following digital rights:
   1) to permit or restrict access to digital records at their discretion and to determine the terms and conditions of such access;
   2) to own, use, and dispose of digital records at their discretion;
   3) to protect their rights by lawful means in the event of unauthorized access to or unlawful use of digital records by third parties.

### Article 15. Digital Resources

1. Digital resources mean organized collections of digital records, as well as applications for creating, processing, storing, accessing, transmitting, and/or distributing digital records.
2. A digital resource identifiable by a domain name or Internet address is a website (online resource).
3. A digital resource in which the creation and processing of digital records are carried out according to common rules using two or more technical means distributed across space and owned by different users of such a resource is a distributed digital resource.
4. Subject to restrictions established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, the owner of digital resources holds the following digital rights:
   1) to permit or restrict access to digital resources at their discretion and to determine the terms and conditions of such access;
   2) to own, use, and dispose of digital resources at their discretion;
   3) to use any designations not prohibited by law for the identification of the digital resource;
   4) to protect their rights by lawful means in the event of unauthorized access to or unlawful use of digital resources by third parties.

### Article 16. Digital Data Exchanges

1. A digital data exchange is a digital platform that provides structured access to digital data, including digital records and digital resources, their cataloging, registration, standardization, and the establishment of terms for access, exchange, and use of such data in accordance with the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. A digital data exchange may be established by:
   1) a state body or state legal entity for the purpose of hosting and providing access to digital data owned by the state, including open government data, anonymized data collected in the course of performing state functions, and other digital data whose access is permitted under the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   2) a legal entity that has obtained a permit to operate a digital data exchange in the manner established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on permits and notifications, for the purpose of organizing access to privately owned digital data or data voluntarily provided by data subjects.
3. Digital data exchanges are required to:
   1) maintain a registry of available digital data and digital rights thereto;
   2) ensure the recording of access conditions, forms of digital rights, and legal restrictions;
   3) comply with approved standards for the quality of digital data and metadata;
   4) ensure the security of storage, processing, and transmission of digital data;
   5) prevent unauthorized access, transfer, and cross-border export of digital data in cases restricted by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. The procedure for the operation of digital data exchanges is determined by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 17. Digital Infrastructure Objects

1. Digital infrastructure objects include digital data processing systems (including data centers and artificial intelligence systems) and telecommunications networks.
2. Relations concerning the creation, placement, and operation of digital infrastructure objects are governed by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. Digital infrastructure objects may be in state and/or private ownership. State digital infrastructure objects are created exclusively for the performance of state functions and the provision of public services.
4. Digital infrastructure objects that have no defined owners and are jointly operated by users are distributed systems.

### Article 18. Digital Systems

1. A digital system is a combination of digital infrastructure objects and digital resources designed for the collection, processing, storage, and dissemination of digital data to support processes in the digital environment, including decision-making.
2. The lifecycle of a digital system includes creation, deployment, operation, modification, maintenance, archiving, and decommissioning, as well as ensuring the preservation of digital data, digital records, and digital objects processed within it.
3. For digital systems used for public purposes or ensuring legally significant interaction, the availability of machine-readable technical documentation reflecting lifecycle stages and component versions is mandatory.
4. The owner of a digital system may, unless otherwise provided by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan or the owner of digital resources, prohibit or restrict the movement and dissemination of digital resources contained in that system.
5. If the owner of a digital system is not the owner of the digital resources contained therein, the procedure for operating the digital system and accessing digital resources is determined by an agreement between the owners. In the absence of such an agreement, priority is given to the access conditions set by the owner of the digital resources.
6. Digital systems that enable interaction among participants in the digital environment according to unified technical and organizational rules form a digital platform.

### Article 19. Digital Platforms

1. A digital platform is a digital system designed to organize digital interaction among participants in the digital environment, facilitate the circulation of digital objects and digital data, record digital events, and carry out actions having legal significance.
2. A digital platform ensures authentication of participants in digital interaction, exchange of digital objects and data, maintenance of digital event logs, implementation of digital rights and obligations, and machine-readability of processes, records, and interaction outcomes.
3. Digital platforms may be used to provide digital services, perform public functions (including in public administration and regulation), account for and circulate digital objects and digital rights, or for other purposes consistent with this Code and the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. A digital platform that enables legally significant actions and supports the operation of state digital infrastructure is subject to registration in the manner established by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.
5. Requirements for digital platforms subject to registration are approved by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.
6. Rules of digital interaction within a platform are defined by its architecture and documented in machine-readable form, ensuring equal access for participants (unless otherwise provided by law), transparency of platform operation conditions, protection of digital data, digital objects, and participants’ digital rights, as well as compatibility with other components of the digital infrastructure.
7. Sectoral legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan applies to services provided by digital platforms, goods, works, or services delivered through digital platforms, and requirements for business users of digital platforms.
8. Relations concerning the production, placement, distribution, and storage of online advertising, as well as the prevention and suppression of illegal content on digital platforms, are governed by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on online platforms and online advertising.
9. Consumer protection legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan applies to public relations arising from the sale of goods, works, or services by digital platforms.

### Article 20. Critically Important Digital Objects

1. Critically important information and communication infrastructure objects (CICIOs) are digital systems, resources, platforms, and infrastructure objects whose disruption or cessation of operation may result in:
   1) loss or unlawful disclosure of personal data, state, official, or other legally protected information;
   2) disruption of state governance, defense, public security, public utilities, or critical economic sectors;
   3) technological, natural, or social emergencies.
2. The list of critically important digital objects, as well as the rules and criteria for classifying digital objects as critically important, are approved by the authorized body in coordination with the National Security Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. Owners of CICIOs are required to:
   1) ensure the operation of objects in compliance with stability and security requirements established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   2) implement organizational and technical measures to ensure information and cybersecurity, including protection against unauthorized access;
   3) conduct security audits in cases provided for by law;
   4) ensure continuous monitoring of object operations and inform authorized bodies of identified threats and incidents;
   5) restrict cross-border data transfers processed on CICIOs in cases established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Non-state digital systems classified as critically important digital objects or intended for the formation of state digital resources are equated to state body digital systems regarding compliance with cybersecurity requirements established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. In cases provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, the owner of critically important digital objects conducts a cybersecurity audit.
6. Information necessary for ensuring the cybersecurity of digital objects is provided to owners of critically important digital objects by the authorized body.
7. Specifics of the creation, operation, protection, and auditing of CICIOs are established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan in the field of information security.

### Article 21. Digital Devices with Network Exchange Functionality

1. Digital devices with network exchange functionality are digital objects technically designed for automatic exchange of digital data with other digital objects using network technologies without direct human involvement in each exchange act.
2. In cases determined by the authorized body, digital devices with network exchange functionality used in public administration, housing and utilities, transport, healthcare, and rights protection may exchange digital data only with trusted digital platforms.
3. Digital devices designed for automated decision-making or affecting physical objects or processes must ensure:
   1) the ability to retain digital records of their actions;
   2) access to digital data created or transmitted by such devices;
   3) compliance with the rights of digital data subjects, including the right to information about device usage.
4. Requirements for the operation of digital devices with network exchange functionality, as well as their classification and regulation procedures, are established by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.

### Article 22. Digital Models

1. A digital model is a digital object containing a formalized representation of the characteristics, structure, behavior, or processes of another object or phenomenon, including physical, digital, legal, and organizational entities.
2. The use of a digital model does not automatically confer legal significance on the modeled characteristics and actions, except in cases expressly provided for by law.
3. Depending on the purpose, digital models may be:
   1) static — reflecting a fixed state of an object or phenomenon;
   2) dynamic — showing changes in characteristics over time or under interaction with other digital objects;
   3) synchronized — updated based on data from external sources or systems in near real-time.
4. A digital model intended for decision-making with legal consequences is subject to registration in the established manner.
5. The procedure for the use, registration, verification, and archival storage of digital models is determined by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.

## Chapter 4. Subjects of Legal Relations in the Digital Environment

### Article 23. Types and Legal Status of Subjects of Legal Relations in the Digital Environment

1. Subjects of legal relations in the digital environment may include individuals, legal entities, and state bodies engaged in the creation, processing, storage, and transmission of digital data.
2. Each subject of legal relations in the digital environment has specific rights and obligations regulated by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan and the rules of digital communities.
3. The legal status of a subject of legal relations in the digital environment is determined by their role and functions within specific legal relations.

### Article 24. Owners of Digital Objects

1. Owners of digital objects are individuals and legal entities who possess digital objects under the right of ownership or other lawful grounds.
2. The owner of digital objects has the right to use and manage digital objects at their discretion within the limits established by legislation and agreements governing the use of such objects.
3. The owner of digital objects is obliged to respect the rights and interests of others related to the use of these objects, including compliance with copyright and related rights, protection of personal data, and confidentiality.
4. In the event of transfer of rights to digital objects to another person, such transfer must be properly formalized in accordance with the requirements of the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. The owner of digital objects is liable for any losses or damage caused by the unlawful use of such objects.

### Article 25. Users of Digital Objects

1. Users of digital objects are individuals and legal entities who lawfully use digital objects.
2. A user of digital objects has the right to use them within the scope of granted rights and in accordance with conditions established by the owner of the digital objects or the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. The user of digital objects is obliged to comply with the terms of use, including requirements for intellectual property protection, confidentiality, and other applicable norms.
4. The user of digital objects is responsible for respecting the rights of the owner of digital objects and third parties, as well as for any actions performed using such objects, including their distribution, copying, modification, and other forms of use.
5. The user of digital objects is obliged to take measures to ensure the security of digital objects, prevent their unlawful use, loss, or damage, unless otherwise provided by contract or legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
6. Users of digital platforms are divided into platform users and business users, depending on their activity on the platform:
   1) business users of a digital platform are individuals and legal entities engaged in entrepreneurial activities for the sale of goods, works, or services through the platform’s service;
   2) platform users are individuals or legal entities using the platform’s services to purchase goods, works, or services.

### Article 26. Data Subjects

1. A data subject is an individual or legal entity to whom digital data processed in the digital environment relate.
2. Individuals are data subjects of personal data.
3. The data subject has the right to protect their data, including the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict processing, and obtain information about the purposes and grounds for data processing.

### Article 27. Digital Communities and Decentralized Autonomous Organizations

1. Digital communities are voluntarily created by their participants in the digital environment on their own initiative. Participants independently determine the rules of such communities.
2. Participants may create digital communities without forming a legal entity or in any organizational and legal form, as well as with any management structure not prohibited by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. When applying the rules of digital communities, state bodies and officials must comply with the requirements of the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Digital communities organized without forming a legal entity and managed by participants through smart contracts without central leadership are recognized as decentralized autonomous organizations.
5. A decentralized autonomous organization is a legally recognized form with limited legal capacity, defined by the terms of the smart contract on which it operates.
6. Participants in a decentralized autonomous organization are subjects of collective governance. Their rights and obligations are determined by the terms of the smart contract.
7. All decisions in decentralized organizations are made through participant voting, with results automatically executed by the smart contract.
8. The liability of participants in a decentralized autonomous organization for actions and decisions made by the organization is limited to their contribution to the organization or as defined by the smart contract.
9. The smart contract defining the governance rules and execution of decisions of a decentralized autonomous organization must be publicly accessible and cannot be changed without participant consent.
10. A decentralized autonomous organization may perform legally significant actions through smart contracts within the limits established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
11. To conduct settlements with third parties, a joint bank account may be opened in second-tier banks for all or several participants of a decentralized autonomous organization.

### Article 28. Artificial Intelligence

1. For the purposes of this Code, artificial intelligence is the functional capability of a digital system to replicate human cognitive functions.
2. Artificial intelligence may be used in the digital environment as a component of digital systems, platforms, and models involved in actions having legal significance.
3. The use of artificial intelligence in the digital environment is permitted provided that this Code and the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on artificial intelligence are complied with.
4. Artificial intelligence is not a separate legal subject.
5. The use of artificial intelligence does not exempt subjects of the digital environment from liability for the consequences of its operation as established by law.
6. The use of artificial intelligence in digital legal relations is permitted provided that its actions are documented, decisions are subject to human review and appeal, and the rights and legitimate interests of other subjects are respected.
7. In cases where actions performed using artificial intelligence have legal effects on a subject, such a subject must be informed of the use of artificial intelligence.
8. In cases provided for by law, the use of artificial intelligence technologies may be restricted or prohibited if they pose a threat to human rights and freedoms, public safety, state secrets, or violate the principles of transparency and manageability of the digital environment.

## Chapter 5. Digital Identification

### Article 29. Right to Identity and the Right to Be Forgotten

1. Subjects of legal relations in the digital environment have the right to identify or not identify themselves, except in cases where the obligation to identify is established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. No one may be compelled to identify themselves, except in cases established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. Owners of digital systems and platforms are obliged, upon request by a subject of legal relations in the digital environment, to delete all digital data and records about such a subject that were voluntarily provided by them, unless otherwise provided by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 30. Identifiers of Digital Data

1. Identifiers and digital records containing them are processed in accordance with the rules established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on personal data, with the specifics provided for in this Chapter of the Code.
2. The unique public identifier in the Republic of Kazakhstan is:
   1) for individuals — the Individual Identification Number (IIN);
   2) for legal entities — the Business Identification Number (BIN).
3. Unique public identifiers are publicly available data processed in accordance with this Code, taking into account the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on personal data and their protection.
4. Identifiers used in state digital resources, as well as other identifiers used for exchanging digital records between digital resources of different owners, are public identifiers. Other identifiers are private identifiers.
5. The choice of identifier to be included in a digital record is made by:
   1) the personal data subject — when data about them are collected by the record owner;
   2) the record owner — when data are created by the owner themselves, including when creating or assigning an identifier;
   3) a third party — when data are obtained by the record owner from a source other than the personal data subject.
6. The owner of digital records is obliged to ensure the uniqueness of identifiers used in digital records.
7. Private identifiers are used and processed in accordance with the internal rules of the digital record owner, provided that the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on personal data protection is observed if such identifiers enable the identification of an individual.
8. The personal data subject has the right to request correction, deletion, or restriction of processing of digital records containing identifiers that enable their identification in case of inaccuracy, violation of processing conditions, or loss of grounds for their use, except in cases expressly provided for by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 31. Digital Authentication Systems

1. Digital authentication is recognized as confirmation of the identity of an individual or the legal capacity of a legal entity in cases where the use of the relevant authentication system is provided for by legislation or agreement between the parties.
2. When applying for digital public services or entering into a transaction, digital authentication is recognized as a proper expression of will to receive a service or conclude a transaction only if a verified second factor is present, understood as biometric authentication, use of a one-time password, token, or other confirmation method distinct from the primary access method.
3. Digital authentication is carried out in accordance with the rules of authentication systems approved by the authorized body.
4. The rules of an authentication system must contain the following provisions:
   1) types of participants in the authentication system;
   2) types of relations in which digital authentication is carried out under this system;
   3) list of identifiers used in the authentication system and methods of their acquisition;
   4) indication of the owner(s) of digital records containing identifiers and authentication results, or indication that such records constitute a distributed digital resource;
   5) authentication methods (methods of obtaining and presenting the key) and the authentication procedure;
   6) procedure for recording digital authentication results and ensuring the integrity of information about digital authentication events;
   7) methods and procedure for publishing or otherwise providing unlimited access to the rules of the authentication system;
   8) prohibition on transferring digital records with identifiers to persons who are not participants in the authentication system, except in cases established by law.
5. Digital authentication in accordance with authentication rules serves as a basis for the creation, modification, or termination of legal relations, including for evidentiary purposes, provided that a second factor is present.
6. Digital records of digital authentication cannot be deemed legally invalid solely on the grounds that they are presented in digital form.
7. Cross-border digital authentication is permitted in cases where an authentication system established outside the Republic of Kazakhstan is recognized as equivalent under international agreements.

### Article 32. Biometric Authentication

1. Biometric data are recognized as personal data and are protected in accordance with the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. Every citizen of the Republic of Kazakhstan has the right to biometric registration for the purpose of digital authentication in accordance with the legislation.
3. The following biometric data of citizens of the Republic of Kazakhstan are subject to processing for authentication purposes:
   1) digital facial image;
   2) fingerprint information.
4. Mandatory biometric authentication of citizens of the Republic of Kazakhstan is carried out only in cases expressly provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, through the State Biometric Identification System. In other cases, a citizen may refuse to use biometric data for digital authentication purposes.
5. The State Biometric Identification System is a digital platform for the processing and storage of biometric data of individuals.
6. The procedure for collecting, processing, and storing biometric data of individuals for biometric authentication is determined by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
7. The storage and processing of biometric data of individuals outside the territory of the Republic of Kazakhstan is prohibited, except in cases expressly provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 33. Digital Signature

1. A digital signature is a set of digital data created by digital signature tools and confirming the authenticity of a digital document, its origin, and the integrity of its content.
2. Codes, passwords, and digital records in distributed ledgers received from digital resources via:
   1) mobile telecommunications subscriber devices, including those registered on the digital government web portal;
   2) instant messaging services;
   3) email;
   4) within a distributed platform,  
may be equated to digital signature tools by agreement of the parties in cases provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. A digital signature is equivalent to a handwritten signature of the signatory and entails identical legal consequences if the following conditions are met:
   1) the authenticity of the digital signature is verified using a public key that has a registration certificate;
   2) the person signing the digital document lawfully possesses the private key of the digital signature;
   3) the digital signature is used in accordance with the information specified in the registration certificate;
   4) the digital signature is created and the registration certificate is issued by an accredited certification authority of the Republic of Kazakhstan or a foreign certification authority registered with a trusted third party in the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Private keys of digital signatures are the property of persons who lawfully possess them. A person may have private keys for different digital systems. Private keys of digital signatures may not be transferred to other persons. Storage of private keys in a certification authority is permitted in accordance with the rules for creating, using, and storing private keys in the certification authority.
5. The head of a legal entity or their deputy may authorize an employee of the legal entity or a designated person, or a digital system, to sign a digital document. Each employee uses a registration certificate and the corresponding private key of the digital signature issued in their name.
6. Digital signature tools are subject to conformity assessment in cases and in the manner established by the technical regulation legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
7. To confirm compliance of a digital signature with the requirements established by this Code, a registration certificate is issued by the certification authority.
8. The activities of the certification authority and its accreditation are regulated by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan on digital documents and digital signatures.

## Chapter 6. Grounds for the Creation, Exercise, and Termination of Legal Relations in the Digital Environment

### Article 34. Creation, Exercise, and Termination of Digital Rights

1. The grounds for the creation, modification, and termination of legal relations in the digital environment are legal facts expressed in digital records, including those presented as digital documents or as part of digital resources.
2. Digital rights arise in cases provided for by this Code by virtue of the creation or acquisition of the corresponding object of relations in the digital environment. No other actions, including registration or notification, are required for the emergence of digital rights.
3. Ownership of digital rights may be confirmed by metadata, including those contained in distributed digital resources. In the event of a dispute over ownership of digital rights to digital records or resources, the burden of proof lies with the owner of the respective digital data or digital resources.
4. Upon alienation of digital rights, including by court decision, they are not terminated but transferred to the new owner.

### Article 35. Digital Documents

1. A digital record whose integrity is ensured by means of a digital signature is recognized as a digital document.  
Persons whose digital signatures are attached to a digital document are considered its authors.
2. A digital document is equivalent to a paper-based document.
3. Digital documents are created, stored, and presented through state and/or non-state digital systems, as well as outside such systems, in the manner established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Digital documents stored in digital systems may be used and presented through services synchronized with such systems for digital documents.
5. Third-party access to digital documents via a digital document service is granted with the user’s consent in the manner established by the authorized body in the field of digitalization.
6. When receiving payment and financial services, identity-verifying digital documents are used by financial and payment organizations and presented by them via the digital document service upon personal appearance (presence) of individuals and verified for authenticity in the established manner.
7. The procedure for collecting, processing, storing, transmitting, searching, disseminating, using, protecting, registering, verifying, and destroying digital documents and other data containing state secrets, using digital systems in secure execution classified as state secrets, as well as the procedure for establishing, accrediting, and terminating the activities of a special certification authority, is determined by the National Security Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
8. Digital documents of state bodies are transferred to state custody in digital archives in the manner established by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 36. Digital Document Standard

1. The digital document standard is a legislatively established format of a digital record ensuring uniformity of structure, content, authenticity verification means, and technical parameters of a digital document.
2. A digital document created and stored in accordance with the digital document standard has the following characteristics:
   - machine-readability;
   - resistance to modification (immutability);
   - verifiability of authenticity through a certification authority;
   - ability to be circulated and stored outside the information system in which it was created.
3. The application of the digital document standard is mandatory for:
   1) state bodies and organizations when creating, transmitting, and storing digital documents;
   2) digital systems intended for the provision of public services and the performance of state functions;
   3) subjects performing legally significant actions with legal consequences, including contract conclusion, rights certification, registration of legal facts, and in other cases provided for by the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Approval and updating of digital document standards are carried out by the authorized body in the field of digitalization in accordance with the technical regulation legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. Non-compliance of a digital document with the established standard does not automatically result in the loss of its legal force if its authenticity and content can be confirmed by another method provided for by this Code or the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 37. Digital Archives

1. Digital archives are digital resources ensuring centralized storage, accounting, systematization, protection, and use of digital documents, digital records, and other digital objects subject to long-term or permanent storage in accordance with the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. The following are subject to transfer to digital archives:
   1) digital documents of state bodies and organizations;
   2) digital documents of legal or historical value;
   3) digital documents certifying the rights of individuals and legal entities;
   4) digital records and resources recognized as significant for public administration, justice, protection of citizens' rights and freedoms, and scientific, social, and cultural memory;
   5) digital systems and platforms in which digital documents subject to archival storage were created and stored, if such systems are recognized as significant in the established manner.
3. Archiving of digital systems includes preserving the technical and logical structure of the digital system, ensuring access to digital records and documents stored in the system, documenting versions and updates of the digital system, and ensuring the ability to reproduce archived data outside the original system.
4. Digital archives and digital objects subject to archival storage must ensure machine-readability.
5. Machine-readability of digital archives means the representation of digital documents, records, metadata, and storage structures in standardized, formalized formats that enable their automated processing, interpretation, search, verification, transmission, reproduction, and long-term storage without human intervention, while preserving legal force and evidentiary value.
6. Machine-readable formats of digital archives must meet established technical requirements ensuring:
   1) independence from specific digital systems and vendors;
   2) openness and reproducibility of data structure;
   3) resistance to technological obsolescence;
   4) compatibility with national and international information systems.
7. The procedure for selection, transfer, storage, use, and destruction of digital documents and systems in digital archives is determined by the authorized body in the field of archival affairs in coordination with the authorized body in the field of digitalization.
8. The authorized body in the field of archival affairs exercises control over compliance with legislative requirements regarding the archival storage of digital objects, including machine-readability in accordance with the requirements of this Code and the archival legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 38. Smart Contracts

1. Subjects of legal relations in the digital environment may, by agreement, express all or part of the terms of such an agreement in a specialized programming language as code, which will subsequently be executed independently of the parties upon receipt of digital data specified in the agreement (smart contract), thereby giving rise to, modifying, or terminating relations between the parties.
2. An agreement providing for the use of a smart contract must contain rules for dispute resolution between the parties, providing for human decision-making in disputes.
3. Requirements regarding the use of smart contracts in specific areas of activity are established by this Code and the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 39. Features of Automated Decision-Making Based on Digital Data

1. Subjects of legal relations in the digital environment may automate decision-making based on digital data so that the results of digital data processing are generated and used to create, modify, or terminate legal relations without human involvement (automated decisions).
2. Persons whose interests are affected by automated decisions have the right to request, and the subject using automated decisions is obliged to review, previously made automated decisions with human involvement.
3. A subject of legal relations in the digital environment using automated decisions is obliged to comply with the requirements established by this Code and the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan regarding automated decision-making in the relevant sectors (areas) of activity.

## Chapter 7. Control and Risk Management in the Digital Environment

### Article 40. State Control over Compliance with Digital Legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan

1. State control in the digital environment is carried out in the form of inspections, preventive control with on-site visits to the subject (object) of control, and preventive control without on-site visits.
2. Inspections and preventive control with on-site visits are carried out in accordance with the Entrepreneurial Code of the Republic of Kazakhstan and the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. Preventive control without on-site visits is carried out in accordance with the Entrepreneurial Code of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 41. Risk Management in the Digital Environment

1. A risk management system, including risks related to the impact on human rights in the digital environment, is developed and implemented by the owner of the object of legal relations in the digital environment.
2. The risk management system in the digital environment defines:
   1) a list of risks, including an assessment of the likelihood and scale of negative consequences;
   2) risk management measures, i.e., measures to reduce the likelihood and scale of negative consequences for each identified risk;
   3) the procedure for evaluating the effectiveness of the existing risk management system and making changes to it based on collected data on its implementation.
3. Additional requirements for the risk management system in the fields of cybersecurity and personal data protection may be established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan for specific digital objects.

### Article 42. Cybersecurity

1. Cybersecurity means a set of legal, organizational, and technical measures aimed at ensuring the protection of digital objects from external and internal cyber threats.
2. The cybersecurity assurance system consists of interacting state bodies, state and non-state organizations, and services implementing measures to ensure cybersecurity in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Informatization” and “On National Security of the Republic of Kazakhstan.”
3. Protection of digital objects is carried out by their owners.
4. Measures for the protection of digital objects are determined by the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Informatization,” unified requirements in the fields of digitalization and cybersecurity, and other legislative acts of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. Unified requirements in the fields of digitalization and cybersecurity are approved by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
6. The provisions of unified requirements in the fields of digitalization and cybersecurity are mandatory for state bodies, local government bodies, state legal entities, quasi-state sector entities, owners of non-state digital systems integrated with state body digital systems, and owners of critically important digital objects.

### Article 43. Testing and Audit of Digital Objects for Compliance with Cybersecurity Requirements

1. Testing of digital objects for compliance with cybersecurity requirements is carried out on a mandatory basis, as well as at the initiative of their owner.
2. Digital objects subject to mandatory testing for compliance with cybersecurity requirements, as well as the procedure for conducting tests, are determined by the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Informatization.”
3. Testing of digital objects for compliance with cybersecurity requirements is carried out in accordance with this Code, the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Informatization,” and the technical regulation legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Audit of digital objects for compliance with cybersecurity requirements is an independent examination of digital objects for compliance with cybersecurity requirements to assess protection against internal and external threats.
   1) the audit of digital objects for compliance with cybersecurity requirements is carried out by individuals and/or legal entities possessing specialized knowledge and experience in cybersecurity and holding a license issued in accordance with the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan;
   2) the audit of digital objects for compliance with cybersecurity requirements is conducted at the initiative of their owner in accordance with the cybersecurity legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan, except in cases provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 44. Information Systems Researcher

1. An information systems researcher is an individual or legal entity conducting analysis of digital objects and information systems aimed at identifying vulnerabilities, architectural shortcomings, and legal and technical risks, including for the purpose of enhancing cybersecurity.
2. Research of digital objects may be carried out:
   1) at the initiative of owners of digital objects or digital systems;
   2) as part of quality audits, cybersecurity compliance audits, or regulatory audits;
   3) under a contract or agreement with authorized bodies;
   4) in other forms not contradicting the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
3. The activities of an information systems researcher are carried out in accordance with the cybersecurity, technical regulation, personal data protection, and state secrets legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
4. Unauthorized access, disruption of operability, or interference with digital objects without the consent of their owners is prohibited, except in cases expressly provided for by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 45. Quality Audit of Digital Objects

1. A quality audit of digital objects is conducted to assess their functionality.
2. A quality audit of digital objects is conducted at the creation, deployment, and operation stages at the initiative of the owner.
3. Audits of digital objects are conducted by individuals and/or legal entities possessing specialized knowledge and experience in digitalization, in the manner determined by the authorized body.
4. Audits of digital objects classified as state secrets are conducted by the National Security Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
5. Audits of digital objects of state bodies and quasi-state sector entities are mandatory, including upon termination of their industrial operation.

### Article 46. Regulatory Audit

1. Regulatory audit is a systematic legal review of existing normative legal acts, their drafts, corporate documents, and internal rules to assess their compliance with the goals and principles of digital development outlined in this Code.
2. Regulatory audit is conducted to evaluate the impact of regulation on the development of the digital economy, ensure technological neutrality and innovative openness of legal norms, identify excessive, outdated, or ineffective requirements, and ensure consistency of legal regulation with national priorities in digitalization.
3. Objects of regulatory audit may include:
   1) draft normative legal acts affecting the digital sphere;
   2) existing legislative and sub-legislative acts, corporate documents, and internal regulations of digital objects;
   3) specific procedures and standards affecting interaction in the digital environment.
4. Regulatory audit is carried out by authorized bodies during lawmaking and law enforcement evaluation, at the initiative of subjects of digital relations, and as part of digital maturity assessments.
5. Based on the results of a regulatory audit, recommendations may be developed for updating or revising normative acts, proposals for legislative amendments, measures to eliminate digital barriers within the state regulatory policy, and conclusions on the compliance of internal documentation with digital transformation requirements.
6. The procedure and methodology for conducting regulatory audits are approved by the authorized body in coordination with the Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 47. Notifications in the Digital Environment

1. Notification of subjects of legal relations in the digital environment is carried out digitally through the Single Notification Point.
2. The Single Notification Point is a state digital resource enabling targeted distribution of notifications from state bodies to digital objects, mobile subscriber devices, and/or email addresses, ensuring guaranteed delivery and storage of information on receipt and reading statuses.
3. Notifications delivered via the Single Notification Point are deemed properly delivered if there is information confirming their reading by the subject of legal relations.

### Article 48. Digital Compliance

1. Digital compliance is a system of internal regulation and control aimed at ensuring compliance with the requirements of the digital legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan during the design, creation, deployment, operation, and decommissioning of digital objects, systems, and platforms.
2. Digital compliance includes identifying applicable requirements of digital legislation and regulatory documents, developing and implementing internal procedures, policies, and standards to ensure compliance, identifying digital risks and implementing risk management measures, internal control mechanisms, monitoring, incident analysis, corrective actions, and interaction with authorized bodies and stakeholders to ensure transparency and trust.
3. Digital compliance is implemented by owners of digital objects, systems, and platforms on a mandatory basis in cases established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan, and on a voluntary basis in other cases.
4. Digital compliance may be based on national and/or international standards, industry recommendations, and may include digital tools and automated solutions for control, analysis, and reporting.
5. Digital compliance rules may be subject to inspection, audit, or digital maturity assessment in accordance with this Code and other legislative acts of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

## Chapter 8. Experimental Regulation in the Digital Environment

### Article 49. Experimental Legal Regime

1. An experimental legal regime is a temporary and limited legal regulation framework, differing from general legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan, applied to innovative projects for testing and adapting regulation.
2. The regime may provide for the temporary non-application of certain legislative provisions, modification of their application procedure, or introduction of new temporary norms, excluding provisions of the Constitution, constitutional laws, and legislation on taxation, budget, customs regulation, environment, subsoil, and subsoil use.
3. The application of an experimental legal regime is permitted only when the project cannot be tested by other lawful means.
4. Participation in the experimental regime is voluntary.
5. Harm caused during the implementation of the experimental regime is subject to compensation in accordance with civil legislation.

### Article 50. Objectives of the Experimental Legal Regime

1. The experimental regime is applied for testing and implementing new digital and technological solutions, creating conditions for innovative projects, reducing legal risks and uncertainty, promoting competition, science, and entrepreneurship, improving the accessibility and quality of goods and services, enhancing the efficiency of public administration, and developing grounds for updating legal regulation.

### Article 51. Principles of the Experimental Legal Regime

1. The experimental legal regime is implemented based on the principles of non-restriction of constitutional rights and freedoms, ensuring the security of individuals, society, and the state, transparency and openness, equal rights of applicants, voluntary participation, and certainty of special regulation in terms of time, scope of persons, and territory.

### Article 52. Limits of the Experimental Legal Regime

1. The experimental legal regime does not apply in the areas of national security, protection of state secrets, defense, anti-corruption, and emergency prevention, as well as to digital objects and systems classified as critically important under the legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. The regime is introduced for a specific group of persons, for a specific period, and within a specific territory.
3. Participants are obliged to inform counterparties of their participation in the experiment and potential risks.
4. The duration of the regime may not be less than one year or more than five years and terminates upon expiration of the established term.

### Article 53. Establishment of the Experimental Legal Regime

1. The decision to introduce an experimental legal regime is made by the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan based on the conclusion of the authorized body coordinating the relevant sector, in coordination with justice, budget planning, antimonopoly, digitalization and cybersecurity bodies, and the Expert Council on Digitalization.
2. Initiators may be both state and private entities.
3. The procedure for initiating and implementing the experimental legal regime is determined by the Government.

### Article 54. Monitoring and Evaluation of the Experimental Legal Regime

1. Monitoring of the regime’s implementation is carried out by the sectoral authorized body.
2. Participants submit reports in the manner established by the Government.
3. Upon completion of the regime, an evaluation of its effectiveness is conducted, which may serve as a basis for legislative amendment proposals.

### Article 55. Amendment and Termination of the Experimental Legal Regime

1. The Government may amend, suspend, or terminate the regime, including upon expiration of the term, at the participant’s request, in the event of liquidation or bankruptcy of a legal entity, or upon identification of risks threatening the rights and freedoms of citizens or the interests of society or the state.
2. The procedure for decision-making is regulated by acts of the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 56. Information Support for the Experimental Legal Regime

1. Information on introduced and initiated experimental regimes is published on the official resources of authorized bodies, including data on implementation results, achieved objectives, and decisions made based on them.

## Chapter 9. Dispute Resolution and Liability for Violation of Digital Legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan

### Article 57. Procedure for Resolving Disputes in the Digital Environment

1. Disputes related to the exercise, modification, or termination of subsoil use rights are subject to resolution in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan and international treaties ratified by the Republic of Kazakhstan.

### Article 58. Liability for Offenses in the Digital Environment

1. Violation of the digital legislation of the Republic of Kazakhstan entails liability established by the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
2. Administrative or criminal liability does not exempt guilty persons from the obligation to remedy the violations committed.
3. Foreign nationals, stateless persons, and foreign legal entities enjoy rights and freedoms and bear obligations in relations arising in the digital environment on an equal basis with citizens and legal entities of the Republic of Kazakhstan, unless otherwise provided by this Code, the laws, and international treaties ratified by the Republic of Kazakhstan.

## Chapter 10. Objectives, Principles, Main Directions, and Forms of International Cooperation of the Republic of Kazakhstan in the Digital Environment

### Article 59. Objectives of International Cooperation of the Republic of Kazakhstan in the Digital Environment

1. The objectives of international cooperation in the digital environment are mutual assistance and coordination of joint actions between the Republic of Kazakhstan and other states and international organizations on issues of digital technology development.

### Article 60. Principles of International Cooperation of the Republic of Kazakhstan in the Digital Environment

1. Cooperation with other states and international organizations is based on the principles of mutual respect, trust, mutual assistance, compromise, non-discrimination, non-interference in each other’s affairs, timely fulfillment of obligations, and peaceful dispute resolution.
2. The state undertakes efforts to create an environment conducive to attracting investment and implementing modern digital technologies based on market approaches to organizing activities in the digital environment.

### Article 61. Directions of International Cooperation of the Republic of Kazakhstan in the Digital Environment

1. The directions of international cooperation of the Republic of Kazakhstan in the digital environment are:
   1) creation, implementation, and development of digital technologies;
   2) internet governance;
   3) elimination of regulatory barriers hindering innovation in the digital environment;
   4) combating cyber threats and cybercrime;
   5) combating digital fraud.
